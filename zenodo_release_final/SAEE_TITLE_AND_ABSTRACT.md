## Abstract

SAEE, Silicon-Amplified Evolutionary Ecology, is presented here as a frozen
empirical phase space object derived from a constrained computational
evolutionary system exhibiting strong attractor convergence and bounded
diversity. The package establishes the public scientific definition of SAEE
without disclosing implementation details.

The reported evidence is observational. A local long-horizon experiment over
100 generations produced a stable population of 8, zero observed collapse
events, a converging fitness-variance tendency, and a lineage DAG with 808
nodes and 1590 edges. Phase-space compression identified `stable_regime` as the
only observed regime, `stable_lineage_basin` as the dominant attractor basin,
and no observed cross-regime transition. Invariant extraction identified
lineage integrity and population viability as local observations, with fitness
convergence and branching-density range as candidate patterns.

The resulting candidate law set contains five falsifiable local laws:
Attractor Dominance, Regime Non-Transition, Lineage Stability, Bounded
Diversity, and Fitness Convergence. These laws are not claimed as universal
laws and are not externally validated. The safe scientific claim is narrower:
under the current constraints, SAEE behaves as a strongly convergent
evolutionary dynamical object rather than an open-ended evolutionary system.
