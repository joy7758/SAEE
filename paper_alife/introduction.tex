\section{Introduction}

Artificial life research studies how life-like behavior can arise in
constructed computational, physical, or hybrid systems. A recurring challenge
is distinguishing a system that continues to generate open-ended novelty from
one that stabilizes into a constrained attractor. SAEE is positioned around
this distinction. It is not presented here as a new mechanism, optimizer, or
runtime. It is presented as a frozen scientific object whose current empirical
phase-space can be interpreted in artificial-life terms.

The object studied in this paper is the Local Canonical Reflexive Evolutionary
Dynamical System Object, or LCR-REDS Object. Its defining features are mutable
operator semantics, lineage-coupled feedback, reflexive observer state, and an
identity constraint that bounds drift. The frozen evidence boundary includes a
stable runtime, local long-horizon observations, phase-space compression, a
candidate law set, a Global State Protocol (GSP), and final architecture rules
that prevent downstream runtime or protocol layers from rewriting the
scientific object.

The central local result is narrow: under the current frozen constraints, SAEE
does not show open-ended evolutionary behavior. Instead, it behaves as a
strongly convergent evolutionary dynamical object dominated by a stable lineage
attractor. This result is not claimed as a universal law. It is a local
empirical interpretation of the current frozen SAEE evidence package.

This paper makes three contributions. First, it presents SAEE as a frozen
reflexive evolutionary dynamical object. Second, it compresses existing
simulation traces into an ALife-readable phase-space interpretation centered
on attractor dominance, bounded drift, and lineage stability. Third, it
describes how GSP and the final architecture contract separate scientific
truth, coordination semantics, and runtime or experimental projections.

\paragraph{Paper type and contribution.}
This paper is a local characterization study, not a universality paper,
benchmark paper, or algorithmic-optimization paper. We study one frozen SAEE
object under fixed kernel constraints and contribute (i) a compact object
specification and (ii) a claim-safe empirical compression of its observed
artificial-life dynamics.
