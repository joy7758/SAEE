We present SAEE as a locally scoped artificial-life object rather than as a
universal theory of evolution. Under a fixed kernel and observer-coupled update
rule, we analyze a frozen empirical phase-space instance with a 100-generation
long-horizon observation surface. Within the reported horizon, the dynamics are
strongly convergent: the system remains in \texttt{stable\_regime}, no collapse
events or cross-regime transitions are observed, and population size remains
bounded. The dominant behavioral basin is
\texttt{stable\_lineage\_basin}, with a long-horizon lineage surface
containing 808 nodes and 1590 edges. A separate Phase II drift surface records
bounded semantic drift with maximum value 0.2 under the repository's existing
drift metric. We use these observations to present a local canonical reflexive
evolutionary dynamical object (LCR-REDS) and to identify five candidate regularities.
These regularities are reported as local, falsifiable candidate
invariants of the present frozen system only; they are not claimed as
universal laws and they are not externally validated. The contribution is
therefore a compact object specification and a claim-safe empirical
characterization of bounded, attractor-dominated behavior in an artificial-life
setting.
