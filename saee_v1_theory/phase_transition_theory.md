# Phase Transition Theory

## Core Idea

An evolutionary phase transition occurs when the qualitative regime of
evolution changes. The state may continue to evolve, but the rule of its
motion, selection geometry, or lineage behavior changes.

## Regimes

Let R_t be the regime classification of state X_t. Possible theoretical
regimes include:

- stable regime;
- exploratory regime;
- chaotic regime;
- collapse regime;
- regenerative regime;
- multi-attractor regime;
- identity-stabilized regime.

A regime is not a single state. It is a qualitative mode of evolution.

## Regime Transition

A phase transition occurs when:

R_t != R_(t+1)

and the change is caused by a shift in at least one of:

- selection pressure geometry;
- mutation operator field;
- lineage topology;
- environment coupling;
- observation feedback;
- identity constraint;
- active evolution dimensions.

## Attractor Formation

An attractor A is a region in state space such that trajectories from a basin
B_A tend to remain in or return to A:

Pr(X_(t+k) in A | X_t in B_A) increases with k

Attractors may be:

- population attractors;
- lineage attractors;
- semantic attractors;
- selection-pressure attractors;
- identity attractors;
- reflexive attractors.

## Collapse States

Collapse occurs when reachable diversity, lineage entropy, or admissible
future states shrink sharply:

|A(X_(t+1))| << |A(X_t)|

or:

H_L(t+1) << H_L(t)

Collapse is not necessarily terminal. It may create conditions for
regeneration.

## Regeneration States

Regeneration occurs when a collapsed or dormant region re-enters the active
state space:

Dormant(X_a) -> Active(X_b)

or when new dimensions, lineages, or pressure fields create renewed
evolutionary capacity.

## Dimensional Emergence

Let D_t be the active dimension set of evolution space. Dimensional emergence
occurs when:

D_(t+1) = D_t union {d_new}

where d_new changes the meaning of variation, selection, or lineage.

Dimensional emergence is a phase transition when it changes the qualitative
regime R_t.

## Critical Thresholds

Phase transitions may occur when a control parameter crosses a threshold:

alpha_t >= alpha_c

Possible control parameters include:

- mutation pressure;
- selection gradient;
- lineage entropy;
- semantic drift;
- reflexive coupling strength;
- identity continuity;
- environmental volatility.

## Phase Transition Principle

Evolutionary systems may change not only in state but in regime. A complete
theory of evolution must therefore describe:

1. state transition;
2. regime transition;
3. attractor formation;
4. collapse and regeneration;
5. emergence of new dimensions of change.
