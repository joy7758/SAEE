# Cover letter — Artificial Intelligence

Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript **“Evidence Presence Is Not Semantic Support:
An Impossibility Result for AI-Agent Readiness Claims”** as a **Research Note**
in *Artificial Intelligence*.

AI agents increasingly act through tools and external resources, so their
evaluation depends on traces, receipts, approvals, and provenance records. The
paper identifies a representation-level failure that applies across agent
architectures: a classifier can recover a semantic claim from an evidence
abstraction if and only if differently labelled packages are separated by
that abstraction. Consequently, a presence-only rule cannot distinguish
relation-valid and relation-invalid packages with identical field-presence
vectors.

The note contributes both a formal result and an executable demonstration. A
16-pair controlled study covers resource authenticity, authorized agent
action, human oversight, and execution boundaries. Every negative preserves
all required fields and the complete JSON key/type shape. Field and structural
rules false-support all 16 negatives; an affirmative-decision ablation still
false-supports 14; a relation-aware evaluator rejects all 16 with localized
reasons. The evaluator, profiles, and positive fixtures pre-date the study and
are pinned by component hashes. The manuscript clearly limits these results
to white-box synthetic construct validation.

We believe this focused result fits AIJ because it provides a general
criterion for the validity of evidence representations used in the evaluation
and production of AI-agent systems, not merely an application report. It also
connects agent provenance research with behavioral and metamorphic testing.

Both authors confirm that the manuscript is original, is not under
consideration elsewhere, and is distinct from the corresponding
author's earlier ALIFE 2026 submission. Data, code, expectations, and full
result manifests are included as supplementary material. OpenAI Codex was used
by the corresponding author during preparation as disclosed in the manuscript;
the corresponding author reviewed and verified the code, references, analysis,
and text. Both authors reviewed and approved the final manuscript and
