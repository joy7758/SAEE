# Abstract

SAEE, Silicon-Amplified Evolutionary Ecology, is presented as a frozen
empirical phase-space object for studying convergent computational evolutionary
dynamics. A local 100-generation observational run shows stable population
size, zero observed collapse events, converging fitness-variance tendency, and
a stable lineage DAG with 808 nodes and 1590 edges. Phase-space compression
identifies `stable_regime` as the only observed regime and
`stable_lineage_basin` as the dominant attractor basin. Candidate law
extraction produces five falsifiable local regularities: attractor dominance,
regime non-transition, lineage stability, bounded diversity, and fitness
convergence. The central claim is bounded: under current constraints, SAEE is
not an open-ended evolutionary system, but a convergent evolutionary dynamical
object with a fixed minimal kernel and stable phase-space structure.
