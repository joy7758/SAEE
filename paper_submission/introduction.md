# Introduction

Computational evolutionary systems are often evaluated by their ability to
generate variation, preserve lineage, and select among alternatives. However,
such systems may converge into stable dynamical structures rather than remain
open-ended. This paper presents SAEE, Silicon-Amplified Evolutionary Ecology,
as a frozen empirical phase space object for studying such convergent
computational evolutionary dynamics.

The goal is not to publish a system implementation. The goal is to define a
scientific object and report its observable dynamics. The current SAEE object
is studied through aggregate experiment summaries, phase-space analysis,
regime classification, attractor mapping, invariant extraction, and candidate
law formulation.

The main empirical result is convergence. Under current constraints, SAEE does
not exhibit open-ended evolution. Instead, it shows a stable regime, a dominant
lineage attractor basin, bounded diversity, preserved lineage topology, and
converging variance.

This positioning separates four layers:

```text
Zenodo = definition rights
paper = explanation rights
GitHub = public abstraction
kernel = private control
```

The paper therefore emphasizes observational science and claim boundaries. It
does not disclose kernel, runtime, mutation, selection, fitness, lineage, or
reproduction implementation.
