# Abstract

SAEE, Silicon-Amplified Evolutionary Ecology, is presented as a frozen
empirical phase space object derived from a constrained computational
evolutionary system. The object exhibits strong attractor convergence, bounded
diversity, stable lineage topology, and measurable regime structure. We report
a local observational study over 100 generations, in which the population
remained stable at 8, no collapse events were observed, fitness variance showed
a converging tendency, and the lineage graph formed a DAG with 808 nodes and
1590 edges.

Phase-space compression identifies `stable_regime` as the only observed
regime, `stable_lineage_basin` as the dominant attractor, and no observed
cross-regime transition. Candidate law extraction yields five falsifiable local
laws: Attractor Dominance, Regime Non-Transition, Lineage Stability, Bounded
Diversity, and Fitness Convergence.

The central conclusion is deliberately bounded: SAEE under current constraints
is not an open-ended evolutionary system. It is a convergent evolutionary
dynamical object with a fixed minimal kernel and a dominant attractor basin.
The paper describes the empirical object and its phase-space structure without
disclosing implementation details.
