## Primary Contributions

1. A synthetic multi-agent benchmark for measuring parasitic transition
   patterns under resource competition, reward or policy drift, and replication
   pressure.
2. Stress testing of `Phi` as one bounded operational transition indicator, with
   entropy and dominance retained as supporting observables.
3. Cross-system evidence across DBI-1, DBI-2, and DBI-3, summarized as an
   empirically observed cross-system consistency within a shared synthetic
   multi-agent modeling framework.
4. Governance heterogeneity: transition emergence is observed across tested
   DBIs, while governance suppression remains architecture-dependent.
5. Reviewer-facing ablation, random-weight, sensitivity, and structural analog
   baseline analyses.

## Reviewer-Safe Interpretation

DBI-3 introduces a distinct interaction topology that reduces transition
alignment while preserving qualitative phase structure. This is a finding, not
a failure.

`Phi` is useful under tested conditions but is not claimed to be unique,
necessary, or superior to all other possible indicators.

Percolation and SIR are structural analog baselines, not competitors. Classical
models such as percolation and SIR also exhibit transition behavior, but under
different generative mechanisms.

## Non-Contributions

The paper does not claim:

- real-world deployment validation,
- production governance readiness,
- a broad theory over all multi-agent systems,
- control guarantees,
- theoretical completeness.

## One-Sentence JAAMAS Positioning

This paper presents a synthetic multi-agent benchmark showing empirically
observed cross-system consistency within a shared synthetic multi-agent
modeling framework, with architecture-dependent governance response.
